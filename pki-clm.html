01 — LANDSCAPE

Every trust decision runs through a key you may not be tracking

Certificates aren't just the padlock in a browser bar. They're the mechanism behind almost every machine-to-machine trust decision in the environment — and most of that population is invisible to whoever only thinks about the public-facing ones.

109 : 1machine identities now exist for every human identity in the enterprise.

Most of those machine identities are backed by a certificate or key — this is the operational substrate underneath that number. Source: Palo Alto Networks, 2026 Identity Security Landscape Report.

02 — EXPOSURE

This isn't hypothetical

Unlike a lot of security risk, a certificate failure doesn't stay quiet. It's binary — valid or expired — and when it fails, it fails in public, all at once.

~32 million UK subscribers, tens of millions more in Japanlost mobile data service for hours on December 6, 2018.

Cause: one expired software certificate in Ericsson network equipment, affecting O2 and its MVNOs (giffgaff, Sky Mobile, Tesco Mobile) in the UK and SoftBank in Japan. Widely reported, December 2018.

147 million people's data, 78 days undetectedin the 2017 Equifax breach.

One internal monitoring certificate expired in January 2017 and went unrenewed for over six months — during which encrypted attacker traffic couldn't be inspected. At the time, Equifax had at least 324 expired certificates across its environment, 79 on business-critical monitoring domains. Widely reported, post-breach findings.

72%of security leaders reported at least one certificate-related outage in the past year.

Source: CyberArk, 2025 State of Machine Identity Security Report. Neither incident above is the exception — it's closer to the base rate.

And the certificates behind both of those named incidents had something in common: nobody had a reliable answer to "which certificates do we actually have, and who owns them?"
